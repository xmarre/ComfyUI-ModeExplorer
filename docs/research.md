# Research and numerical contracts

Native ComfyUI Krea 2 Raw and Turbo use a single-stream DiT and the constant flow prediction contract. Krea's [reference inference code](https://github.com/krea-ai/krea-2/blob/main/sampling.py) uses velocity, device-generated Gaussian noise and Euler. ComfyUI usually draws noise on CPU and runs the requested solver. Krea's guidance uses v_cond + g(v_cond − v_uncond), equivalent to ComfyUI CFG of 1 + g. Turbo distillation, CFG and solver steps are separate experimental variables.

## Feature edits

Disjoint rectangular cells cover the padded image-token grid. Partial-edge cells use their actual area in the pooling adjoint. The hook changes positive generated-image attention coefficients and retains text, reference and negative CFG coefficients at the hook. Later blocks can exchange information with edited tokens. The window and Krea block 1 are provisional settings.

The bounded update maintains the coarse spatial mean. The verified update maintains the token-weighted mean before native rounding, applies the local norm bound and an 8% pooled global RMS cap, and chooses the first decrease in the measured pooled objective at step scales 1, 1/2 or 1/4. Its reference rows stay fixed. The search scores only the analytic objective on coarse averaged features. The cap can reduce edit magnitude compared with the bounded update. Model-output fidelity and anatomy require separate measurements.

Equal positive conditioning defines the eligible cohorts. The cosine objective takes log-mean-exp of eligible pairwise similarity. The RBF Gram uses a 0.001 diagonal ridge and a guarded Cholesky solve. The volume derivative forms pair differences before weighting by inverse-Gram entries, avoiding cancellation at exact duplicates. With one competitor, temperature and bandwidth change only the scalar coefficient of a tangent gradient. That scalar can still affect the baseline saturation function. ModeExplorer retains the pair values of 0.5 and 0.55 and exposes the controls for collective weighting with three or more valid members. Median bandwidth uses the eligible collective distances and clamps the result to [0.25, 1.25].

## Reference and initialization paths

The eight-slot bank remains the default. Larger banks generate slots on demand, and the per-seed strategy derives separate streams from the full seed and reference index. Each actual step uses up to the configured trajectory count until the invocation's shared reference budget is spent. A budget of four and two trajectories permits two probes on two actual steps. The closest-reference policy picks the largest cosine relative to the live feature with deterministic tie order. References share the positive condition, timestep, weight patches and image layout. Batch-dependent attention can round differently when positive and negative CFG rows are evaluated together in the live path.

First-estimate continuation uses the first clean estimate for later shadow states. Rolling Euler uses each shadow's own latest measured velocity. Contiguous deterministic Euler probes therefore follow independent positive-only Euler trajectories up to native precision. Skipped evaluations produce larger Euler intervals. With ER-SDE the shadow is a deterministic approximation that uses none of the live solver's multistep coefficients or RNG.

Initialization uses a fixed projected-clean pooling grid of up to 4×4 and fixed reference noise slots 0 and 1 for every seed. The sequential reference and feature grid settings do not affect initialization. The resulting population bias has not been measured. The first proposal rotates the original Gaussian noise by an RMS chord of strength × 0.18 along a stop-velocity surrogate. The trial budget counts that first proposal and up to two smaller retries. The real predicted-clean map can amplify that chord, and the relative metric depends on baseline clean RMS. Initialization rejects invalid baselines. It scores a candidate on a real guider prediction, requiring log-volume gain above 0.0001 and relative clean-latent RMS displacement at most 0.32 − 0.27 × fidelity. On an overshoot, the next radius uses 80% of the estimated boundary, bounded to between 1/16 and 1/2 of the previous radius. Other rejections halve the radius. The first accepted row stays accepted. A poorly aligned direction can still fail every trial and leave the row's original noise intact. If the 45-second launch deadline is reached, same-seed initialization may depend on runtime. The reason appears in the diagnostics. For native ER-SDE and SA-Solver the first guider coordinate follows the native solver's adjusted sigma, while input noise scaling follows KSAMPLER.

## Forecast regimes and anatomy

Guarded Spectrum starts fresh history at a feature-regime transition. Full Spectrum keeps forecast eligibility and existing history. Its later forecasts can combine retained anchors computed before and after the attention edit. The per-dispatch trace identifies anchors with edit attempts. An attempt may be gated out. Operator changes introduce a possible forecasting error even at a matched sigma. Compare Full, guarded and an all-actual sham/active panel to separate routing and feature effects.

Use planner and final images to locate when anatomy changes. Lowering the adaptive threshold changes both which pairs are eligible and the strength ramp. An activation-distance improvement cannot identify the cause of a duplicated limb, or establish a gain in diversity, anatomy or identity at production resolution.

### Weak guidance and structural sensitivity

In bounded feature mode, the scalar rate contains the product `strength × (1 − 0.85 × fidelity)`, multiplied by a feature-dependent saturation term and, when enabled, the adaptive gate. With initialization disabled and all other settings held fixed, strength and fidelity therefore do not provide independent feature-magnitude axes. At strength 0.03 and fidelity 0.70, the nominal rate envelope is 0.01215; this is not a measured relative attention displacement. Later evaluations depend on the trajectory changed by earlier edits.

Exploratory full-checkpoint comparisons found that small early edits can redirect an inconsistent limb orientation, while intermediate strengths can produce less coherent transitions. Another matched image pair showed improved local object and garment coherence with largely retained framing; those differences were visible at the planner stage, along with finer apparent hair-strand separation near the hairline. The early pooled edit has no explicit texture-enhancement objective; detail differences can emerge through subsequent denoising. A separate pilot reported a structural defect with a four-reference budget that disappeared when reduced to two. These observations motivate testing strength and the guidance window independently. They do not establish a universal threshold, a favorable anatomical attractor, or an improvement in aggregate image quality. The budget is a cap on auxiliary forwards, not a direct time-window setting; its effect depends on eligibility and trajectory count.

The guidance objective contains no anatomical or semantic quality classifier. A reduction in feature similarity can redirect later model predictions toward either a more or less coherent result. Evaluate both recovered native failures and newly introduced failures on an unselected paired panel.
